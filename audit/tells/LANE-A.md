## Top five recurring patterns (lane-wide counts)

1. **The negation family** — ≈116 instances ("not X, but Y" / "It is
   not X. It is Y." / "No X, no Y, no Z" and kin), of which perhaps
   five are TONE-sanctioned calibration seeds and a dozen more are
   content-defensible corrections. Worst: ch09 (18), ch08 (16), ch10
   (14), ch07 (13). This is the lane's signature tell: whole sections
   are architected as negation-then-affirmation (ch02 §2.4's threefold
   "a char is not X"; ch10's closing section opens and closes on the
   pivot).
2. **Em-dash as default connective** — 458 em-dashes in ≈34,400 prose
   words = **13.3 per 1000 lane-wide**, three to five times a K&R
   baseline, with most spent on reflexive hinge-dashes (where a comma,
   colon, or period serves) and dash-pair insertions rather than
   deliberate breaks. Only ch01 (9.0) and how-to-read (8.1) approach a
   defensible rate.
3. **The section-close epigram/maxim cadence** — 30+ rowed instances;
   every chapter ends most sections (and many paragraphs) on an
   aphorism ("Most bugs should be so polite", "It is a name for a
   brace", "the lexer holds the line so the runtime never has to").
   TONE's calibration models the device once per passage; the lane
   deploys it as the default landing gear, which is what makes the
   register read composed rather than plain.
4. **The honest-motif** — ≈20 instances of the book notarizing its own
   candor ("the honest answer", "the honest order", "the honest
   comparison", "this book does not make measured claims without the
   measurement", "the cost is stated rather than hidden"), plus two
   chapter titles and a section title on "honestly". The candor is
   real; the certification of it is the tell.
5. **The "worth X-ing" meta-formula** — ≈15 instances ("worth stating
   plainly", "worth taking literally", "worth naming", "worth
   memorizing"), five in ch10 alone — throat-clearing that announces
   importance instead of showing it; the self-ranking family ("the
   chapter's largest claim", "the sentence to keep", "the sentence of
   this section") is its sibling with ~10 more.

## SYSTEMIC — template-baked, one bs15 fix each, not thirty

1. **Schedule narration / deferral prose** (charter). Ten rows, one
   pattern: prose that teaches the gap between book and toolchain —
   "yet", "today", "at this printing", "has (not) landed", "the pinned
   interpreter", "until the interpreter's char work lands". ch05 carries
   nine rows, ch02 one (§2.4's compiler-lane note). One editorial
   policy — state scope, never schedule; gaps go to the ledger —
   retires all ten. This is the lane's only charter-class defect that
   is systemic rather than local.
2. **Cross-chapter template sentences.** Whole formula-sentences recur
   across chapters as if pasted from one authoring prompt:
   - the measurement-credo ("this book does not make measured claims
     without the measurement: chapter 21 runs the comparison, prints
     the numbers CI produced, and names the cases where C still wins")
     — ch02 491-494 ≈ ch03 466-469, near-verbatim;
   - "Credit where it is due/owed" — ch06 395, ch08 1031, ch11 225;
   - "The cost, stated plainly" — ch08 1021, ch11 160;
   - "The shape to keep/build" — ch06 390, ch08 819, ch10 168;
   - "Neither X is free" — ch01 210, ch02 262, ch06 129;
   - the negative-space opener ("notice/look at/count what is not in
     it") — ch03 50, ch08 115, ch10 61;
   - the 3 a.m./3am failure — ch06 129, ch08 1015;
   - "N X, one Y" numeric parallels — ch01, ch03 ×2, ch08 ×2, ch11.
   One bs15 pass with this list in hand keeps the best single instance
   of each and rewrites the rest; nothing else de-templates the book.
3. **The cross-reference appendage** — "— §N.M does X" / "which is
   §N.M's business" bolted onto sentence ends via dash or relative
   clause (ch01 ×5 rowed, plus ch02, ch03, ch04, ch06, ch10). One
   convention — cross-refs become parentheses or their own plain
   sentence — fixes the lane.
4. **The dash-pair-enclosing-a-list shape** — «X — a, b, c — Y»
   (notation.md ×3 rowed as the template's origin; ch02 226-229, ch03
   140-148 and 219-225, ch10 146-149 and 171-173). One rule: lists go
   in parens, colons, or their own sentence.
5. **Bolded rule-sentences in running prose** — ch04 382-384, ch07
   374-375, ch08 120-121 and 413-414, ch09 109-110, often introduced by
   "the rule is one sentence". Either TONE ratifies bold-rule-sentences
   as a book convention (they are arguably good pedagogy) or bs15
   unbolds them; the current state is an unratified template.
6. **The section-close maxim cadence** (pattern 3 above) is also
   systemic rather than local: ch07's catalog shows all eight sections
   closing on a moral. bs15 should break the cadence in half the
   sections per chapter rather than polish each maxim.
